## Abstract

This thesis is an introduction to the geometric modelling of problems in quantum
computing. Quantum states are represented either as pure states in the projective
Hilbert space $P(H)$, or more generally by density operators, while quantum logic
gates correspond to transformations in $U(2^n)$. Within this setting, the thesis
examines the relationship between quantum computing, quantum information geometry
and quantum groups in the form of Hopf algebras.

Central objects include the symmetric logarithmic derivative and the quantum Fisher
matrix, examined alongside their classical counterparts; the Fubini-Study metric as
the Kähler metric of the projective Hilbert space, seen as the real part of the
quantum geometric tensor; and ZX-calculus, whose rewrite rules are related to the
axioms of Hopf algebraic structures, allowing quantum circuits to be viewed as
objects of Hopf algebras.

## Contents

**1. Preliminaries** — differential geometry, Lie groups, probability and statistics;
Hopf algebras and F-Hopf algebras; the small quantum group $u_q(sl_2)$

**2. Quantum computing** — qubits, density operators and quantum logic gates; quantum
versus classical algorithms; universality of CNOT, the no-cloning theorem and the
threshold theorem

**3. Quantum information geometry** — classical information geometry and its quantum
counterparts; the quantum information matrix and the symmetric logarithmic derivative;
the quantum geometric tensor and the Fubini-Study metric

**4. ZX-calculus and Hopf algebras** — representing and simplifying quantum circuits;
the correspondence between ZX rules and Hopf algebra axioms
